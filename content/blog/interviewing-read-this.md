After roughly 3 months of navigating through 10+ job interviews, I've gathered some facts and thoughts. Let me walk you through them.

## The Job Descriptions

- **"Required Skills" are Often Negotiable:** Companies list their dream candidate, not their realistic one. If you match 60% of the "must-haves," apply anyway.
- **The "Good to Have" Gold Mine:** These skills are your ticket to standing out. Acquire one or two before applying, and you'll leap ahead of the competition.
- **Nothing in the Job Description is Extra:** If you can touch on all the requirements a little, then you are good to go.

## Pre-Interview

- **Stalk (Professionally) on LinkedIn:** Find your potential team members. What skills do they have that you lack? Cold message them if necessary, show your effort.
- **Show Initiative & Solve Problems:** Demonstrate genuine interest in the company. Research their challenges and come prepared with potential solutions. Remember, companies want problem-solvers who can boost their bottom line. Action is louder than talking. Identify a challenge the company is facing (through news, social media, or their products) and come to the interview with a solution sketch. I've seen candidates get hired on the spot for this level of initiative. Companies are always looking for candidates that can help them resolve problems.
- **Quality Over Quantity:** Instead of applying everywhere, focus on roles where you see long-term potential. Tailor your applications to showcase how you're the perfect fit for each specific position. Though I do know the math theory behind applying to a large number of jobs, and eventually landing one or two, make sure you are really willing to work for certain companies. I always go to Glassdoor and screen the first layers. Usually, higher-rated companies are great.
- **Amend Your Resume Specifically:** Based on your research, write the job description you think the company needs, not just what they've posted. Present this vision if asked, "Do you have any questions for us?"

## The Interview: It's a Two-Way Psychological Game

Interviews are not just Q&A sessions; they're complex dances of psychology:

- **Prioritize Communication:** Even in technical interviews, your ability to articulate ideas clearly is crucial. Employers value candidates who can collaborate effectively.
- **Stay Humble and Attentive:** Listen carefully to the interviewer's guidance and ask for clarification when needed. This shows engagement and critical thinking.

## The Post-Interview Mindset

Your actions after the interview can be just as crucial as the interview itself.

- **Assume Nothing:** While hoping for the best, mentally prepare for rejection. This approach minimizes personal disappointment and keeps you proactive in your job search.
- **Not Necessary to Follow Up:** Why? Think from the employer's perspective; they wouldn't want to miss a candidate that fits, hence they will get back to you ASAP. Currently, 100% of the interview opportunities that I have followed up (cause they don’t get back to me in the promised timeframe) are rejected. Then I realize that:

> **Great companies do provide feedback and reasoning in the promised timeframe, good companies tell you that they have moved on to the next candidates on time, worst one they will simply ignore and don't bother to get back to you. It's kind of frustrating, I know.**

## The Long Game: Building Interview Immunity

Develop a mindset that turns the job search process into a tool for personal growth:

- **Treat Interviews as Free Consulting Sessions:** Each interview is a chance to learn about different companies and industries. Accumulate this knowledge to position yourself as an industry insider.
- **Reject the Rejections:** Reframe rejections as redirections. Each "no" is guiding you toward the right "yes." Keep a "rejection collection" and celebrate it growing – it means you're pushing your boundaries.
- **Correct Mindset is Powerful:** Try to add the stuff you want to achieve with a "yet".

> I haven't been accepted by a great company "yet"
> I am not a suitable candidate for them "yet"
> You get the idea.

## Final Thoughts

I have been actively searching for jobs for 3 months now, and each rejection does cause my confidence level to go lower. But I know I should not be feeling that, because I'm improving, and I should always move on and move forward.

> Pro-tip: Then I go seek advice and consolation from ChatGPT. Here's what I feel really helps me:

- **Reflect on your progress:** Reaching the final stage of an interview shows you're qualified. Many people don't get that far, so acknowledge how far you've come.
- **Recognize it as a learning experience:** Every interview offers valuable lessons. Identify areas where you can improve, and use this feedback for future opportunities.
- **Remember, one interview doesn't define your worth:** A single failure does not define your capabilities. Many successful people have faced rejection before succeeding.
- **You're not alone:** Everyone faces rejection at some point in their career. It's a universal experience, and each rejection brings you closer to success.
- **Analyze what went well:** Think about the positive aspects of the interview. Recognizing your strengths can boost your confidence and serve as a reminder of your capabilities.
- **Reframe rejection:** Instead of seeing it as a failure, view it as redirection—perhaps this wasn't the right fit, and something better is waiting for you.
- **Talk to mentors or peers:** Discussing your experience with someone else can provide perspective, advice, and encouragement.
- **Keep honing your skills:** Use the experience to continue learning and improving your skill set. This shows resilience and determination, which are key to long-term success.
- **Visualize your success:** Picture yourself succeeding in future interviews. Positive visualization can have a powerful impact on your mindset and boost your self-belief.
- **Stay proactive:** Keep applying and preparing for other opportunities. Action fosters confidence, and every new step forward is progress.

Finally, I wish y’all good luck with your interview!!
