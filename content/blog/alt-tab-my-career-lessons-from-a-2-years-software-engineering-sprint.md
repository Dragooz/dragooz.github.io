## The Leap into Python Software Engineer

Two years ago, I dove headfirst into the world of Python full-stack development. I was eager, driven, and ready to conquer the tech world. Little did I know how much I’d learn — not just about coding, but about myself and the industry as a whole.

As I gained experience in LLM and Django, particularly in crafting prompts for language models and writing business logic while exposing APIs, I found myself at a crossroads. The company was great, but something was missing. I craved more — more diversity, more precision, more impact, more growth. So, I made the tough decision to resign.

Why? It wasn’t just one thing. I felt the constant push for speed was compromising the quality of our work. I wanted to build products that could stand the test of time, not just meet the next deadline. Plus, I had this burning desire to create something that could help many, not just a select few.

## Embracing AI as a Teammate

Here’s something that might surprise you: AI isn’t just a tool — it’s becoming our teammate. Imagine having a tireless assistant who can help debug your code, generate test cases, or even suggest optimizations. That’s the power of AI in software development.

I remember the day this really hit home. I was struggling with a particularly nasty bug, spending hours combing through lines of code. On a whim, I decided to describe the problem to an AI model. Within minutes, it pointed out a logical flaw I’d completely overlooked. It was like having a senior developer looking over my shoulder, but one that was available 24/7.

This experience taught me a valuable lesson: embracing AI doesn’t mean replacing human creativity — it means amplifying it. By leveraging AI, I could focus on the bigger picture, on designing elegant solutions rather than getting bogged down in implementation details.

Another crucial point is that the era of coding as we know it is evolving. As AI models become stronger in the coming years, programming syntax will no longer be the primary blocker. Instead, it’s problem-solving skills and industrial experience that will truly matter.

## The Art of Asking Questions & Simplifying Business Logic

When I first started as a developer, I believed that success was all about building solutions and solving problems through coding. But over time, I’ve realized that true success lies in asking the right questions — especially when things get complicated.

I remember working on a project to develop an order management system. Given the requirements, I immediately dove into coding, eager to get things done. My initial approach was to loop through the bots, constantly checking for new orders to assign to them. This seemed to work well at first.

But as the project progressed, more requirements piled up. We needed to handle scenarios like prioritizing VIP orders for VIP bots or ensuring that if no VIP bot was available, a normal bot could step in. As the logic grew more complex to meet these new demands, so did the code, and our problems started to multiply. The solution became more elusive by the day.

That’s when our tech lead posed a simple but powerful question: “What if we flipped the logic? Instead of assigning orders to bots, what if we assigned bots to orders?”

This small shift in perspective changed everything. By restructuring the logic to align better with the system’s needs, we eliminated unnecessary complexity and arrived at a much cleaner and more efficient solution.

That experience taught me that the real key to problem-solving often lies in challenging your own assumptions. Sometimes, the most valuable debugging tool isn’t in your code — it’s your ability to step back, rethink your approach, and simplify the logic. It’s a blend of technical skills and business insight that I aim to master in the future.

## New Journey: Building Your Personal Tech Empire

I officially resigned on October 4th, 2024. Of course, I feel insecure at times, but that’s just part of the growth process, right?

Here’s the biggest lesson I’ve learned: your career is more than just a job — it’s your own personal tech empire. Every project, every bug, every late-night debugging session is an investment in your future.

My to-do list isn’t just tasks — it’s my product roadmap. Those tough problems I used to dread? They’re now opportunities to add features to my skill set.

But here’s the kicker — it’s not just about technical skills. I’ve learned that my unique perspective as a young developer with a foot in both traditional programming and AI is valuable. So, I’ve started documenting my journey, sharing my insights, and connecting with others in the field.

As I look towards my next role, I’m excited. Not just for the new challenges, but for the opportunity to build products that can make a difference. Because at the end of the day, that’s what being a software engineer is all about — using our skills to create something meaningful.

## The Next Move

If you’re in the same position as me — recently resigned and contemplating your next move — here’s my thoughts:

1. **Embrace the uncertainty**: This transition period is an opportunity for growth and self-discovery.
2. **Leverage AI**: Don’t just use AI tools; learn to collaborate with them as you would with human colleagues.
3. **Focus on problem-solving**: Hone your ability to simplify complex problems and ask the right questions.
4. **Build your personal brand**: Share your unique insights and experiences. Your perspective is valuable.
5. **Stay curious**: The tech world is ever-evolving. Keep learning and adapting.

Remember, your career is your own personal GitHub repository. Each experience is a commit, each project a pull request. It’s time to merge your passions with your skills and see what amazing things you can build.

What’s your next commit in the GitHub of your career? The pull request is open — it’s time to push your changes and make your mark on the world of technology.
