Before ChatGPT, to prepare for software engineering interviews, I still recall grinding on LeetCode, reading books like Cracking Coding Interviews and System Designs Interview, and watching mock interviews on YouTube. Now it seems like everything has changed, cause the tools we have access to have been upgraded.

What used to take me hours to find answers for now takes minutes, with accuracy, via the power of LLMs. Remembering those “textbook answers” is no longer an advantage when presenting yourself, IMO. So the question is, what is?

From my view, it’s problem-solving skills and the ability to understand value:

- How would you approach this problem? How do you break it down? How do you solve it? If given A and B, what would you pick, and why?

It’s no longer about how many frameworks you know, or how much syntax you can recall (yeah, it used to be cool to write code from scratch and see it run straight away). All of that can be generated by AI already. Now it’s time to actually understand a business, whether you’re aligned with its mission and vision, and your ability to solve problems for the company.

In the interview, it all comes down to one keyword: company growth. If you’re able to provide value to the company by solving their headaches / core problems / blockers that you are great at, then you’ll have a higher chance of getting hired.

The main thoughts on my mind at this stage (software engineer x 4years)?

**Ask yourself, if I were to join the company:**

- What problems can I solve for them?
- How can I help the company grow / provide value?

Then based on that, personalize what you wanna share.

**About own personal learning journey:**

- Can I develop skills over time, doing the job?
- Will I get a chance to be exposed to real experience?
- Most importantly, will I enjoy it?

Ok now, to further speed up your pre-interview prep, AI can be utilized depending on what kind of questions you ask. You could create your own unique set of questions to ask it, so it can do the stuff and give you short answers. Understand them, and you should be good.

Examples:

- Paste in the company website and ask: What’s the company’s business model? What are its mission and vision? What problem is it solving?
- What does the team I’m joining look like?
- What does the day-to-day job look like?

The answers are, of course, not 100%, but at least you’ll have something to clarify with the interviewers already.

Also:

- What kind of projects will I be involved in?
- Past projects: any that could be shared, and how long did they take?
- What are the expectations of me? Say, at 3 months, 6 months, etc.

Then, something personal you’ve realized from your own experience, e.g.:

- How much budget is there for claiming AI tool usage?
- How wide is the field I can be exposed to on the job? E.g., whether I’d get exposed to different pillars of the engineering team, serving different purposes, etc.

These are all my POV on startups. For big companies, I’m not too sure. I had one experience (yeah, I got the “Thank you for your time” email), and my two cents: they’re still hiring candidates who know strong tech concepts (textbook answers), so you can’t go in with experience alone. You have to study like you’re preparing for an exam. Not wrong, but ok.

That’s my thought being software engineer for 4 years, and I’m still liking it so far. Hope it’s valuable insights for you, whether you are a fresh grad, seniors, or even bosses looking to hire engineers👍
