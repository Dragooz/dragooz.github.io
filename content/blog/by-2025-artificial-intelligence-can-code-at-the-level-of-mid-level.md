Yea. Mark Zuckerberg, the founder of Meta says that in the recent blog.

When I came across articles like this, I was mind-blown. Just 6 years ago, I was struggling to understand programming. I vividly remember battling with Java, posting a question on Stack Overflow, and getting scolded for how I phrased it (Well deserved, even the current me feels like smacking myself for it 🤦. Here’s the infamous post: [https://stackoverflow.com/questions/56014777/please-review-these-code-it-doesnt-work-like-i-expected](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/56014777/please-review-these-code-it-doesnt-work-like-i-expected))

Back then, the struggle was real:

- I didn’t know about or have access to programming books.
- YouTube tutorials lacked the depth I needed.
- Stack Overflow often too professional and hard to understand, especially if you are working on un-common problem.
- And I had no network of senior developers to turn to for guidance.

Fast forward to today: I copy-pasted the same question I posted years ago into AI tools like ChatGPT, and not only did I get answers, but they were explained patiently. This is truly an era that fosters curiosity and learning, free from the blockers I once faced.

AI doesn’t judge you for asking a “stupid” question. It doesn’t scold or discourage (though, yes, it occasionally hallucinates — but that’s unintentional). It provides answers tirelessly (as long as you’ve paid for the subscription, of course; that’s why I’m even working on my own small project to leverage API calls so I can rely on AI without ongoing subscriptions to save some money x)).

Back in the day, I often wondered: How could basic concepts like declaring constants, understanding for loops, or writing if-else statements lead to creating software that could change the world? Today, the answer is simple: Just ask “why” repeatedly on Google — or better yet, tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and others — and you’ll find the answer. If I’d had access to these resources six years ago, my learning curve would’ve skyrocketed.

But here’s the flip side:

It’s so easy to access knowledge now that the gap between those who stay curious and those who slack off will grow rapidly. AI is empowering, but it’s also a double-edged sword. It can help you learn and grow exponentially, but it can also replace you at work if you’re not constantly upskilling.

Some reflections after this journey:

1. **AI is here to stay.** For the next 10 years (and beyond), it will become increasingly integral to our lives. Learn it, or risk being left behind.
2. **Take it one step at a time.** The anxiety to learn everything is real, but good things take time. This is a marathon, not a sprint.
3. **Reevaluate your current job.** What can you learn or do that makes AI your tool instead of your replacement?

With all the advancements I’ve seen — and just three years of involvement in AI that feels like decades of progress — I can’t help but wonder:

If we do reach AGI, what will the future look like for humanity? Is a utopia on the horizon? 🤔

Well, that’s whole new discussion for another day I guess!
